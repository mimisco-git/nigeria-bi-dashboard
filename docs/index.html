📈
GDP Recovery is Sustained
After contracting by -1.92% in 2020 (COVID + oil shock), Nigeria's economy has staged a consistent recovery. Q3 2024 recorded real GDP growth of 3.46% — the highest since 2014 for that quarter — driven by Services sector growth of 5.19%. The economy has grown every quarter since Q1 2022.
SOURCE: NBS GDP Q3 2024 (Table 9) / World Bank GDP growth series
🛢️
Oil: Export King, GDP Minority
Crude petroleum accounts for 62.89% of Nigeria's total export value (Q1 2025), yet contributes only 5.57% of real GDP (Q3 2024). This structural mismatch shows extreme export concentration in a capital-intensive sector employing few Nigerians, while non-oil activities drive 94.43% of domestic economic output.
SOURCE: NBS Trade Statistics Q1 2025 (Product Ranking) / NBS GDP Q3 2024 (Table 8)
🌾
Crop Production: The Unsung Champion
Crop production is the single largest GDP activity in Nigeria at 26.51% of real GDP (Q3 2024). Agriculture as a whole contributes 28.65% of real GDP and employs the most Nigerians. Yet it receives a fraction of the government investment directed at the oil sector, representing a major policy gap.
SOURCE: NBS GDP Q3 2024 — Figure 2 / Table 7 (% Distribution at 2010 constant prices)
💳
Finance Sector: Explosive Growth
Finance & Insurance recorded 30.83% real growth YoY in Q3 2024 — by far the fastest growing major sector. Financial Institutions alone grew at 31.92%. This reflects Nigeria's ongoing fintech transformation, with mobile banking, digital payments, and financial inclusion driving the formalisation of a historically cash-heavy economy.
SOURCE: NBS GDP Q3 2024 — Table 4 (Real GDP Growth at Constant 2010 Prices)
📡
Telecom is the Digital Backbone
Telecommunications & Information Services contributes 13.94% of real GDP — the 3rd largest single activity in the economy — growing at 5.92% in Q3 2024. Nigeria's digital economy increasingly depends on this sector, and its outsized contribution relative to its employment base signals strong future multiplier effects.
SOURCE: NBS GDP Q3 2024 — Table 7 / Table 4
🔥
Inflation: The Persistent Threat
Consumer price inflation surged from 9.01% in 2015 to 24.66% by 2023, driven by fuel subsidy removal, Naira devaluation, and food supply shocks. High inflation erodes real incomes, deters investment, and disproportionately hurts Nigeria's lowest-income households who spend the most of their income on food.
SOURCE: World Bank Open Data — Inflation, consumer prices (annual %) · Nigeria · 2015–2023
