Data-Driven Analysis
Key Economic Insights
All findings derived directly from NBS and World Bank official data
📈
GDP Recovery is Sustained
After contracting by -1.92% in 2020, Nigeria staged a consistent recovery. Q3 2024 recorded real growth of 3.46% — the strongest Q3 since 2014 — driven by Services sector growth of 5.19%. Every quarter since Q1 2022 has seen positive growth.
NBS GDP Q3 2024 (Table 9) / World Bank
🛢️
Oil: Export King, GDP Minority
Crude petroleum is 62.89% of Nigeria's exports yet only 5.57% of real GDP. This structural mismatch shows extreme export concentration in a capital-intensive sector that employs few Nigerians. Non-oil activities drive 94.43% of domestic output.
NBS Trade Q1 2025 / NBS GDP Q3 2024 (Table 8)
🌾
Crop Production: Unsung Champion
Crop production is the single largest GDP activity at 26.51% of real GDP. Agriculture as a whole contributes 28.65% and employs the most Nigerians, yet receives disproportionately low investment relative to the oil sector.
NBS GDP Q3 2024 — Figure 2 / Table 7
💳
Finance Sector: Explosive Growth
Finance & Insurance recorded 30.83% real growth YoY in Q3 2024 — by far the fastest growing major sector. Financial Institutions grew at 31.92%, reflecting Nigeria's ongoing fintech transformation and digital banking boom.
NBS GDP Q3 2024 — Table 4
📡
Telecom is the Digital Backbone
Telecom & Information Services contributes 13.94% of real GDP — the 3rd largest single activity — growing at 5.92% in Q3 2024. Its outsized contribution relative to employment signals strong future multiplier effects for Nigeria's digital economy.
NBS GDP Q3 2024 — Table 7 / Table 4
🔥
Inflation: The Persistent Threat
Inflation surged from 9.01% in 2015 to 24.66% by 2023, driven by fuel subsidy removal, Naira devaluation, and food supply shocks. High inflation erodes real incomes and disproportionately hurts Nigeria's lowest-income households.
